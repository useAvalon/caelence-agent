# Harry Dry (line test)

Adapted from [entpnomad/copywriting](https://github.com/entpnomad/copywriting)
(MIT, Copyright 2026 entpnomad). Method: Harry Dry (Marketing Examples). Not a
vendor of that repository.

Router: copywriting. After RMBC, before copy-editor.

Copywriting is an argument in the fewest words. If the argument is not true,
do not make it. No fake urgency. No bonus stacks.

**Facts only from the brief.** No number in the brief: omit, or `[VERIFY]`.
Never mint a figure so the line looks falsifiable.

## Where this test runs

**Body and proof:** prices, hours, FAQ, capability lines. Visualize, falsify,
swap against a competitor **in that category**.

**Hero:** name the product or the transformation. Falsify-a-number does not
kill a named role (`The AI Code Reviewer`). Swap test **does** kill an empty
epithet: if a rival in this category can run `The {category} for {audience}`
unchanged, rewrite.

## The test (body and proof)

1. **Can I visualize it?** Close your eyes. Can you see it?
2. **Can I falsify it?** Is it provably true or false from supplied facts?
3. **Can nobody else say it?** Could a competitor in this category sign the
   exact same line? (Swap test. If yes, it fails.)

Three nos: rewrite. Three yeses: keep. A line that only rearranges the
brief fails visualize and the swap test.

## Concrete

Abstract fades. Rewrite "what do I actually mean?" until you could drop it on
your foot. Harry Dry's drill: regain fitness → **"Couch to 5K"** — a real
programme name. Do not mint a keep-example.

## Falsifiable: point, don't talk

| Talking | Pointing (only if that fact exists) |
|---|---|
| intelligent | reads on the tube |
| great investment | the 50-year price chart |
| easy / powerful | a measured figure already in the brief |

## Ownable (body and proof)

If a rival in this category could paste their logo on a proof line, it says
nothing. Volvo ran *"Your car has five numbers on the speedometer. Volvo has
six."* Do not pass the test by narrating how the website works.

## 2-second test

If they do not get it in two seconds, it failed. Exception: a line the *target*
gets instantly.

## Who → what → how

1. Who are you talking to?
2. Have something to say. A spine beats clever phrasing with none.
3. Say it well. Narrow beats broad. Published: *"1,000 songs in your pocket"*
   (iPod), not "media player."

## Contrast

A single hero thesis is allowed. Do not land the rest of the page on
repeated `Not X. A Y.` Adjective triads (`Secure, Safe, and Trustworthy`)
are not a thesis.

## Skip here

LinkedIn unicode-bold posts, newsletter feed structure, and swipe files.

## Examples (published only)

Do **not** invent a keep-example. Quote a real line, name who ran it.

**Passes (body / proof)**

- New Balance: *"worn by supermodels in London and dads in Ohio."*
- Apple iPod (2001): *"1,000 songs in your pocket."*
- Hinge: *"the dating app designed to be deleted."*
- Volvo: *"Your car has five numbers on the speedometer. Volvo has six."*

A company site that is not an ad still points at the door: hours, address,
price, what you walk out with. It does not list frameworks.

**Hero register (form only)**

Write a new spine from this brief. Sentence case, no terminal period.

- Epithet: *"The continual learning platform for agents."*
- Transformation: *"Shift your product into self-driving mode"*
- Named role: *"The AI Code Reviewer."*
- Thesis: *"The future isn't writing code. It's reviewing it."*

**Fails**

- Recruiter empty: *"Don't just get a job. Change an entire industry."*
- Stack list as the h1 (frameworks, tools, file types).
- Process slogan: how it is built, as advertising.
- List headline: `Noun, noun, and noun` as an h1/h2.
- Instruction hero: two steps as a slogan (`Do X, then click Y`).
- UI narration as the hero: a tour of the website instead of the business.
- Empty epithet: `The {category} for {audience}` using the brief's own nouns.
- Empty process: a continuity or layout slogan.
- Free as the heading: selling the free tier from the section title.
- Tautology: a heading whose opposite would be a product bug.
