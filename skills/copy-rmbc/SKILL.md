# RMBC (adapted)

Adapted from [Stefan Georgi's RMBC](https://github.com/coleschaffer/copywritingskills-rmbc)
(MIT, Copyright 2026 Stefan Georgi). Not a vendor of that repository. Do not
install the 42 DR skills. Do not run gates that require fake proof.

Router: copywriting. Next: copy-harry-dry.

Copy thinking before writing. Do not start at the headline. A facts brief
is research input, not finished copy.

## 1. Research

From this project's spec, sitemap, and brief only:

- What it is, who it is for, the primary action
- Facts: names, hours, prices, place, product, URLs
- What the reader already knows. Do not invent a failed-past story.

If a fact is missing, omit it or ask. Do not fill the hole with a plausible
number, town, or customer.

## 2. Mechanism

Why it works, in the reader's terms. **Internal**, not the hero.
Not a feature list. Not a fake proprietary name. Not a quantified engine
that was not measured.

A bakery's mechanism is how they bake. A SaaS product's mechanism is how
the product works. Neither belongs in the h1.

## 3. Brief

One positioning spine. Prefer named role, transformation, or a single
thesis. Epithet only when X is not the category. Not a feature list. Not
a tour of the website. Proof = only what was supplied. CTA = a verb that
matches the state it produces. No scarcity clock, bonus-stack math, or
assumed guarantee.

## 4. Copy

Hero is the spine: one positioning line, sentence case, no terminal period.
Subhead is one line. Section titles sound like that company's page, not a
mechanic with a drumroll, not a continuity slogan. Two CTAs per section
maximum. Oxford comma. Present tense. Restating the brief is a fail.

Then run Harry Dry on body and proof. Swap-test the hero if it is
`The {category} for {audience}`. Do not use a missing number to kill a
named role.

## Gates (inverted)

The upstream RMBC gates demand a number, testimonial, and urgency on every
claim. Those require invented proof. Here:

1. **Specificity from the brief.** A number, name, or timeframe only when
   supplied.
2. **Honest mechanism.** How it actually works. No fake named system.
3. **Audience from the brief.** Do not invent what they tried and what failed.
4. **Proof that exists.** A real price, hour, address, or name is enough.
   Missing testimonials is correct.
5. **Objections with facts.** What they get, what it costs if priced. No fake
   deadline.

Unproven claims: omit or `[VERIFY]` in a draft. Never a fake figure.

## Dropped

VSL, cart abandonment, supplement research, scarcity as a default,
soap-opera sequences, bonus stacks, 5-10x value-comparison pricing,
assumed guarantees, required social-proof blocks.

## Examples (published or supplied facts)

**Pointing with a real figure:** Stripe's site states *$1.9T in payments
volume processed in 2025*. That is a published number. *"Powerful payments
infrastructure"* is talking. Do not invent "12,000 teams".

**A local business:** hours and the address from the brief. Not a 5-star
row you made up.

**Hero register (form only, not a line to paste):** named role *"The AI Code
Reviewer."* Transformation *"Shift your product into self-driving mode."*
Thesis *"The future isn't writing code. It's reviewing it."* Epithet only
when X is not the category: *"The continual learning platform for agents."*
Fail: *The {category} for {audience}* restated from the brief. *"Secure,
Safe, and Trustworthy AI"* is a weak triad, not the craft.

Write a new spine from **this** brief.
